# Transparency and Explainability

Transparency is the practice of making the purpose, scope, and limitations of an AI system understandable to stakeholders. For users, this may mean explaining when AI is involved, what inputs it uses, and what decisions might be affected by its output.

Explainability is the ability to provide a human-understandable rationale for a model's decision, especially in cases with legal, medical, financial, or employment consequences. This can take the form of feature attribution, counterfactual explanations, or a summary of the main signals that influenced the recommendation.

AI teams should document model assumptions, known failure modes, and the contexts in which the model should not be used. This helps users and decision-makers judge when the model should be treated as a decision aid rather than as an autonomous authority.

Source: Responsible AI Handbook, model documentation guidance.
